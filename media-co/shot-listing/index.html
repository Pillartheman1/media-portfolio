Shot listing

How I write a shot list and a script for commercial work, before anyone is in front of the camera.

I write a hyper-detailed shot list and a script in Notion for every project, including the commercial and brand work on this side of the site. The point of the page is to turn creative intent into precise written direction: a precise visual description of shot composition and framing, what the lens is doing, how the camera moves, and what the light is doing at that time of day, written with the same attention to detail I want in the final frame.

On a product film that written spec is what I shoot from, and what I use afterward to evaluate the footage — checking continuity from setup to setup, whether the pacing of a process sequence still holds, and whether the shots have temporal coherence when they are cut together. Visual storytelling here is often about material and light; the descriptions are written so they can be followed without a conversation filling in the gaps.

The mockups below are an example shot list for a generic luxury-brand product film, mixing a tailoring process with jewelry macro work. They are not a record of a client from this site, and they do not use a client's name.

For examples of how I look at footage shot by shot after the fact — composition, lens, movement, lighting, and why a shot works — see Cinematography breakdowns, a set of long-form videos from the Klabunde Media Co. YouTube channel.

Example — script to shots

The work is in the cloth, and in the last thing you put on.

  1. 01 WS, dolly in, 35mm

    The cloth arrives; the line can start here, under the rumble of the roll.

  2. 02 ECU, rack, 100mm

    A cut in for the weave and the chalk, no dialogue needed.

  3. 03 MS, gimbal arc, 50mm

    The jacket finds the shoulders; the first half of the line sits here.

  4. 04–05 CU / ECU, macro

    The ring, then the facet. The second half of the line, quieter.
