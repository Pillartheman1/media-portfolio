Shot listing

How I write a shot list and a script before I shoot, and how I use that page once the footage is in.

Before I pick up a camera I translate the creative intent of a piece into a written shot list and a script. I do this in Notion, for every project: the personal work on this side of the site, and the client work at Klabunde Media Co. The lists are prompt-ready shot descriptions: a precise visual description of each setup — framing, lens, movement, light, duration — written with enough attention to detail that I can execute it without guessing, and so the footage can be checked against what was planned.

Visual storytelling, for me, starts on that page. A list is where shot composition gets decided, where camera movement is chosen for a reason, and where I can see whether a sequence will hold together. After the shoot I come back to the same document to evaluate the footage against a written spec: does the continuity hold, does the pacing match what I wrote, and do the shots still have temporal coherence when they sit next to each other.

The mockups below are an example shot list for a short personal narrative — a dusk departure, then a quiet return — not a project from this site. They show the level of detail I write to. For a look at a real pre-production file, including the full shot list and script, see I deleted Instagram. On the company side I also publish long-form cinematography breakdowns, including shot-by-shot visual evaluation of footage; those live on Klabunde Media Co. — Cinematography breakdowns.

Script to shots

One line from a script, mapped to the setups that cover it.

Example — script to shots

I left the house later than I meant to, and the light was already going.

  1. 01 MS, static, 35mm

    The line begins here, still in the doorway, before any walk.

  2. 02 CU, tilt down, 50mm

    The first steps; no dialogue, just the move out of the house.

  3. 03 MS, gimbal push-in, 28mm

    The rest of the line, walking, eyeline off-lens.

  4. 04 WS, static, 35mm

    A hold after the line, looking back, so the thought can land.
